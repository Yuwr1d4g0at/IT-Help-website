Wi-Fi Not Working?

The steps to try, in order, when the internet stops working at home. Print this and stick it on the fridge, since you won't be able to look it up online when the Wi-Fi is down.

1. Is It Just You, or Everyone?

  • Try another device on the same Wi-Fi, like a phone or a tablet. If nothing in the house can get online, the problem is the router or the line, not your computer.
  • Check for an outage in your provider's app or on their support line (MEO, NOS, Vodafone, DIGI, or whoever you pay). If there's a fault in your area, restarting things at home won't help; you just have to wait.

2. Restart in the Right Order

  • Turn the router off at the power button or the plug, and leave it off for 30 seconds.
  • Turn it back on and wait until the lights settle, usually a few minutes. Don't touch anything while it's still blinking through its startup.
  • Then restart your device, once the router is fully back up. Doing it in this order means your device reconnects to a fresh router.

3. On the Device Itself

  • Check that Wi-Fi is switched on and airplane mode is off. Both are easy to toggle by accident.
  • Make sure you're on your own network, not a neighbour's or a guest network with a similar name.
  • Forget the network and join again, typing the password in fresh. The password is often printed on a sticker on the router itself.
  • Check the password carefully: capital letters, zeros and the letter O, and the number 1 and the letter l are the usual mix-ups.

4. Weak or Dropping Signal

  • Move closer to the router and see if it improves. Thick walls, floors, and metal all weaken the signal.
  • Keep the router out in the open, up high and near the middle of the house, not inside a cupboard or behind the TV.
  • Watch out for the microwave. If the Wi-Fi drops every time it's running, that's the cause, and it comes back once it stops.
  • Know your two networks. Many routers show two names, one ending in 2.4G and one in 5G. The 2.4 GHz one reaches further and through walls; the 5 GHz one is faster but only up close. If one is bad in a far room, try the other.

5. Quick Fixes on Windows

  • Run the network troubleshooter. Search for Troubleshoot in the Start menu and run the one for network and internet. It fixes the simple things on its own.
  • Try a network reset as a last step: Settings, Network & internet, Advanced network settings, Network reset. It removes every saved Wi-Fi network and password, so have your Wi-Fi password to hand before you do it. The computer restarts afterwards.

6. Who to Call

  • Call your provider if no device in the house can get online after a router restart, if the router's lights show an error, or if there's an outage in your area.
  • Call me if everything else connects fine and only one computer won't, or if the Wi-Fi is weak in part of the house and you'd like it sorted properly.
