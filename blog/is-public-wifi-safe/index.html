Is Public Wi-Fi Safe? What Actually Matters

Home / Tips / Is Public Wi-Fi Safe?
October 2026

Public Wi-Fi gets a scary reputation. The truth is more boring: it's much safer than it used to be, but there are still a few real traps worth knowing about.

What's mostly a myth now

Almost every website and banking app today uses encryption (the padlock, or "https"), so someone sitting on the same cafe Wi-Fi can't simply read what you're doing. The movie scene where a hacker watches your passwords fly by is mostly out of date.

The real risks

  • Fake hotspots. A network called "Free_Airport_WiFi" or "Cafe_Guest" can be set up by anyone. If you connect to the fake one, the person running it sees your traffic and can try to send you to fake login pages.
  • "Log in to continue" pages that ask for too much. A sign-in page asking for your email, phone number, or worse, a card number "to verify you're human", is a red flag.
  • Your device connecting by itself. Phones and laptops remember networks and rejoin anything with a familiar name, including a fake one with the same name.
  • Out-of-date devices. An unpatched laptop or phone is easier to attack from the same network than an updated one.

Five habits that cover it

  1. Ask the staff for the exact network name and password, and don't join similar-looking ones.
  2. Turn off auto-join for public networks, and "forget" them when you leave.
  3. Do banking and anything sensitive on mobile data. Your phone's own connection is safer than any shared network, and it's one tap away.
  4. Pay attention to warnings. If your browser shows a certificate or "not secure" warning, stop. Don't click through it.
  5. Keep your phone and laptop updated, and turn on two-factor authentication on your important accounts, so a stolen password alone isn't enough.

What about a VPN?

A reputable VPN helps in one specific way: it hides your traffic from whoever runs the Wi-Fi. It doesn't make a fake website real, it doesn't stop you typing a password into a phishing page, and a bad VPN can be worse than no VPN at all. If you travel a lot, a good one is a sensible extra, and I list one I trust on the software page. For everyday use, the five habits above matter more.
